About Me

I am Cristian Borja, a physicist and PhD candidate at Freie Universität Berlin. My research interests lie in condensed matter physics, quantum materials, and the study of electronic and optical properties of hybrid materials.

I obtained my Master of Science in Physics from Universidad de los Andes in Bogotá, Colombia, where I worked on superconductivity and charge density waves in an extended 1D Fermi-Hubbard model with disorder.

Before starting my doctoral studies in Berlin, I was involved in research at the University of Antwerp, working on the filling of carbon nanotubes with electron donor and electron acceptor molecules, as well as their characterization using different spectroscopy techniques: resonant Raman and UV-vis-NIR spectroscopy.

Research

Currently, I work as a PhD student within the CRC1772-Mol2DMat, developing new hybrid systems combining molecular systems and two-dimensional and one-dimensional materials.

My research focuses on the encapsulation and study of one-dimensional molecular systems inside nanotubes (carbon nanotubes and boron nitride nanotubes) as a way to promote one-dimensional ordering of molecular systems and investigate confinement effects in low-dimensional materials.

My previous research involved the comparison of techniques and approaches for filling carbon nanotubes with electron donor and electron acceptor molecules, as well as the assessment of molecular doping effects.

Publications

My scientific contributions include studies on molecular filling and doping strategies for carbon nanotubes, including the comparison of different approaches for introducing electron donor and electron acceptor molecules.

I have also contributed to the development and calibration of a muon hodoscope designed to measure muon flux and its dependence on zenith angle in Bogotá. This work aims toward future applications of muon radiography for density characterization of large geological objects.
